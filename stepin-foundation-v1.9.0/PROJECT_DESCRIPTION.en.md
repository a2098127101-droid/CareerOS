# StepIn Project Overview

StepIn is a practice-first system for university students with little or no internship experience. It is designed for students who do not yet know what real jobs look like day to day, where to start, or which direction may fit them.

StepIn does not begin with courses, job labels, or career tests. It begins with one real but simple work task that a beginner can understand and complete.

The core progression is:

**Start doing → Follow a scaffold → Do it independently → Revise from feedback → Try again in a new context → Combine small tasks into a project → Build capability across tasks → Explain what you can actually do.**

## Foundation first

Before professional specialization, students practice a shared set of foundational capabilities:

- understand a task;
- find key information;
- organize information;
- compare options and make judgments;
- identify problems;
- explain reasoning;
- produce a clear deliverable;
- revise from feedback;
- transfer a skill to a new situation;
- explain what they did.

The system records how much help was used, whether work was completed independently, whether the same capability transfers to another context, and whether repeated performance across different tasks supports the same capability.

## From small tasks to projects

Beginners are not expected to understand “projects” before they have done real work. StepIn first gives them small tasks such as reading materials, organizing information, spotting a problem, prioritizing options, or writing a simple recommendation. These tasks are later connected into a coherent mini-project.

Professional workspaces such as spreadsheets, candidate screening, interview coding, issue handling, research boards, and prioritization appear later, after the student has built a common practice foundation.

## Practice before specialization

Career direction is deliberately delayed. Students first experience several kinds of work and build a record of what they can actually do. Professional paths then emerge from practice rather than from a one-time questionnaire.

Courses, knowledge resources, methods, and professional content support the practice process. They are not the product itself.

## Evidence of capability

StepIn links work actions, revisions, new-context attempts, artifact versions, and teacher feedback. A capability is not treated as mastered because a student completed one exercise. Confidence grows when the student performs the same underlying capability across different tasks and situations.

The long-term goal is for each student to be able to answer:

- What have I actually done?
- What can I do independently now?
- What can I still do when the situation changes?
- What work can I show?
- How can I explain this experience clearly in a resume or interview?

## Current status

StepIn Foundation v1.9.0 includes a foundational capability model, beginner task runtime, fading scaffolds, cross-task capability aggregation, task-to-mini-project composition, practice-expression support, delayed career-path exposure, and integration points with the existing Practice, Evidence, Artifact, and Teacher Review systems.

The current GitHub production branch also contains CareerOS production capabilities such as Domain Intelligence, security hardening, deployment infrastructure, and locked CI. Foundation v1.9.0 is therefore being integrated through a dedicated branch and Draft PR instead of replacing the production line.

## Intended users

StepIn is designed primarily for students who have no internship experience, do not yet understand what real work looks like, are unsure where to begin, or have learned coursework but have not yet turned that knowledge into practical capability.

The goal is not to make students merely look experienced. The goal is to help them move from not knowing how to start to being able to do the work, improve it, repeat it in new situations, and explain what they have actually accomplished.
